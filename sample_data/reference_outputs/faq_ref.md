# Frequently Asked Questions: India's National AI Strategy 2024

**Source Document:** National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2024 Update (NITI Aayog, June 2024)
**Verification:** All answers cite specific source locations (Page, Section) for traceability

---

## 📋 GENERAL QUESTIONS

### Q1: What is the total budget for India's AI Mission and over what period?
**A:** The AI Mission has a total outlay of **₹10,300 crore** over **five years (2024-2029)**.
*Source: Page 1, Executive Summary; Page 23, Chapter 4, Section 4.1 (Investment Table)*

### Q2: How does this strategy differ from the 2018 National Strategy for AI?
**A:** The 2024 update incorporates lessons learned since 2018, addresses the generative AI revolution, establishes sovereign compute as a strategic imperative, allocates dedicated funding (₹10,300 cr vs no dedicated budget in 2018), and aligns with DPDP Act 2023 for data governance.
*Source: Page 2, Chapter 1, Section 1.2 (India's AI Journey So Far); Page 2, Chapter 1, Section 1.1 (Global AI Landscape)*

### Q3: What are the seven pillars of the AI Mission?
**A:** The seven pillars are: (1) Compute Infrastructure, (2) Foundational Models, (3) Datasets, (4) Applications, (5) Skilling, (6) Governance, and (7) International Partnerships.
*Source: Page 3, Chapter 1, Executive Summary; Pages 12-22, Chapter 3*

### Q4: Who governs the AI Mission implementation?
**A:** A Mission Directorate under the CEO of NITI Aayog, with quarterly financial reviews by an Empowered Committee and annual independent audit by CAG.
*Source: Page 24, Chapter 4, Section 4.3 (Financial Governance)*

### Q5: How is the ₹10,300 crore allocated across pillars?
**A:**
| Pillar | Allocation | % of Total |
|--------|------------|------------|
| Compute Infrastructure | ₹4,564 cr | 44.3% |
| Foundational Models | ₹1,971 cr | 19.1% |
| Datasets | ₹826 cr | 8.0% |
| Applications | ₹1,240 cr | 12.0% |
| Skilling | ₹689 cr | 6.7% |
| Governance | ₹412 cr | 4.0% |
| International Partnerships | ₹598 cr | 5.8% |
*Source: Page 23, Chapter 4, Section 4.1 (Investment Table)*

### Q6: What is the funding split between Centre, States, and Industry?
**A:** Central Government: 60% (₹6,180 cr), State Governments: 20% (₹2,060 cr - matching grants), Industry Consortium: 15% (₹1,545 cr - matched funding), International/Other: 5% (₹515 cr).
*Source: Page 23, Chapter 4, Section 4.2 (Funding Mechanism)*

### Q7: How much is allocated for AI compute infrastructure specifically?
**A:** **₹4,564 crore (44.3% of total budget)** for establishing 10,000+ GPU sovereign compute cluster delivering 100+ PFLOPs by 2029.
*Source: Page 23, Chapter 4, Section 4.1; Page 12, Chapter 3, Pillar 1*

## 🏥 HEALTHCARE AI

### Q8: How will AI improve healthcare access for ordinary citizens?
**A:** AI diagnostics (TB screening, cancer detection, retinal scanning) will be deployed in **50,000 Health & Wellness Centres by 2029**, with vernacular language support across 22 languages. This addresses the current 1:834 doctor-patient ratio (WHO norm: 1:1000).
*Source: Page 7, Chapter 2, Section 2.1 (Healthcare AI); Page 26, Chapter 6, KPI Table*

### Q9: Is my health data safe with AI systems?
**A:** Yes. All AI systems must comply with the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023**, which mandates consent, purpose limitation, data minimization, and strict penalties for violations. Health data is classified as sensitive personal data with enhanced protections.
*Source: Page 18, Chapter 5, Section 5.3 (Data Governance); Page 17, Chapter 5, Section 5.1 (Principles - Privacy)*

## 🌾 AGRICULTURE AI

### Q10: How will farmers benefit from the AI Mission?
**A:** **10 crore farmers** will receive AI advisories by 2027 (baseline: 2 crore) covering precision farming, pest prediction, price forecasting, and climate resilience. Services delivered via mobile apps in local languages through Kisan Call Centre (1800-180-1551).
*Source: Page 8, Chapter 2, Section 2.2 (Agriculture AI); Page 26, Chapter 6, KPI Table*

### Q11: Will AI replace farmers or agricultural workers?
**A:** No. The strategy explicitly states AI is for **augmentation**, not replacement. AI provides decision support (crop advisories, soil analysis), while human expertise remains central. Reskilling programs are funded (₹689 cr) for any workforce transitions.
*Source: Page 17, Chapter 5, Section 5.1 (Principle 8: Human Agency); Page 14, Chapter 3, Pillar 5 (Skilling)*

## 🎓 EDUCATION & SKILLING

### Q12: What AI education will my children receive in school?
**A:** **Mandatory AI literacy from Grade 6** onwards, with personalized learning content in 22 scheduled languages. Curriculum integration from school through PhD levels, with industry apprenticeships and faculty exchange programs.
*Source: Page 9, Chapter 2, Section 2.3 (Education and Skilling); Page 14, Chapter 3, Pillar 5*

### Q13: How many AI professionals will be trained and by when?
**A:** Target: **10 lakh AI professionals by 2029** (baseline: 4.16 lakh in 2024). Includes reskilling programs for displaced workers and global talent attraction through AI Research Chairs.
*Source: Page 9, Chapter 2, Section 2.3; Page 26, Chapter 6, KPI Table*

## 🏙️ SMART CITIES & GOVERNANCE

### Q14: Which cities will get AI-enabled command centres?
**A:** **100 smart cities** will have AI command centres for traffic management, waste optimization, public safety, and service delivery by 2029.
*Source: Page 9, Chapter 2, Section 2.4 (Smart Cities); Page 26, Chapter 6, KPI Table*

### Q15: How will AI improve my interactions with government services?
**A:** Chatbots for municipal services, automated grievance redressal, faster certificate issuance, AI-assisted benefit targeting (reducing exclusion errors in Direct Benefit Transfer), and 24/7 citizen helplines in multiple languages.
*Source: Page 10, Chapter 2, Section 2.5 (Governance); Page 19, Public Advisory Reference*

## 🛡️ PRIVACY, ETHICS & RIGHTS

### Q16: Can the government use AI to monitor citizens?
**A:** **No.** The strategy explicitly prohibits mass surveillance. Principles include: Inclusivity, Transparency, Accountability, Privacy, Security, Fairness, Human Agency, and Sustainability. High-risk AI requires conformity assessment and registration.
*Source: Page 17, Chapter 5, Section 5.1 (Principles); Page 18, Chapter 5, Section 5.2 (Regulatory Approach - Risk-based classification)*

### Q17: What if an AI system makes a wrong decision affecting me?
**A:** You have the **Right to Explanation** under the governance framework. High-risk AI decisions must be explainable and auditable. A dedicated grievance portal (CPGRAMS: pgportal.gov.in) and Data Protection Board (dpb.gov.in) handle complaints. DPDP Act 2023 provides legal recourse.
*Source: Page 17, Chapter 5, Section 5.1 (Transparency, Accountability); Page 18, Chapter 5, Section 5.2; Page 19, Public Advisory Reference*

### Q18: How is bias prevented in government AI systems?
**A:** **Mandatory bias audits** for all public sector AI systems. Algorithmic transparency requirements for high-risk AI. Sectoral regulators (Health: CDSCO, Finance: RBI, Telecom: TRAI) oversee domain-specific compliance.
*Source: Page 17, Chapter 5, Section 5.1 (Fairness); Page 18, Chapter 5, Section 5.2 (Regulatory Approach)*

### Q19: Can I opt out of AI-based government services?
**A:** **Yes.** The strategy mandates that non-AI alternatives must be available for all government services. Citizens can choose human-processed pathways.
*Source: Page 17, Chapter 5, Section 5.1 (Human Agency); Page 19, Public Advisory Reference*

## 💻 TECHNICAL & INFRASTRUCTURE

### Q20: What is "sovereign compute" and why does it matter?
**A:** **10,000+ GPUs owned and operated in India** (not rented from foreign cloud providers), delivering 100+ PFLOPs by 2029. This ensures strategic autonomy, data sovereignty, and affordable access for Indian researchers/startups. 50% renewable energy mandate by 2027.
*Source: Page 12, Chapter 3, Pillar 1 (Compute Infrastructure); Page 23, Chapter 4, Section 4.1*

### Q21: Will India build its own Large Language Models?
**A:** **Yes.** The strategy allocates **₹1,971 crore** for developing **5+ Indic-language LLMs** (22 languages, 100B+ parameters) plus domain-specific models for healthcare, agriculture, legal, and finance. Open-source release with responsible use licenses.
*Source: Page 13, Chapter 3, Pillar 2 (Foundational Models); Page 23, Chapter 4, Section 4.1*

### Q22: How will datasets be made available for AI development?
**A:** A **National Data Platform** will publish **500+ curated, annotated, rights-cleared datasets** by 2029 (baseline: 50). Sectoral data exchanges for Health, Agriculture, Education, Finance. Synthetic data generation for sensitive domains.
*Source: Page 13, Chapter 3, Pillar 3 (Datasets); Page 26, Chapter 6, KPI Table*

## 🌍 INTERNATIONAL & ECONOMIC

### Q23: How does India's AI strategy compare globally?
**A:** India joins USA (CHIPS Act), EU (AI Act), China (Next Gen AI Plan) in sovereign AI investment. Unique aspects: Population-scale deployment focus, 22-language inclusivity, democratic governance framework, Global South leadership via GPAI. Global AI market projected at **$1.8T by 2030** (37.3% CAGR).
*Source: Page 2, Chapter 1, Section 1.1 (Global AI Landscape); Page 22, Chapter 3, Pillar 7*

### Q24: What international partnerships are planned?
**A:** Active in **Global Partnership on AI (GPAI)**, bilateral cooperation with US, EU, Japan, Israel, UAE. Participation in ISO/IEC JTC1 and BIS standardization. Development assistance: AI for Global South.
*Source: Page 16, Chapter 3, Pillar 7 (International Partnerships)*

### Q25: How many AI startups will be supported?
**A:** Target: **2,000 AI startups incubated by 2029** (baseline: 200). Support includes subsidized compute credits, challenge grants, public procurement preference for Made-in-India AI, and regulatory sandbox access.
*Source: Page 14, Chapter 3, Pillar 4 (Applications); Page 26, Chapter 6, KPI Table*

## 📊 MONITORING & ACCOUNTABILITY

### Q26: How will the public know if the AI Mission is working?
**A:** **Quarterly public dashboards**, annual independent evaluation (third-party), mid-term review in 2027 with course correction, citizen satisfaction surveys (annual), and economic impact studies (biennial). CAG audits annually.
*Source: Page 24, Chapter 4, Section 4.3 (Financial Governance); Page 26, Chapter 6, Section 6.2*

### Q27: What are the key success metrics?
**A:**
| Metric | 2024 Baseline | 2029 Target |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| AI Compute (PFLOPs) | 5 | 100+ |
| Indic LLMs Deployed | 0 | 5+ |
| Curated Datasets | 50 | 500+ |
| AI Professionals | 4.16 lakh | 10 lakh |
| Farmers Reached | 2 crore | 10 crore |
| Health Centres with AI | 5,000 | 50,000 |
| AI Startups | 200 | 2,000 |
| AI Patents/Year | 1,200 | 10,000 |
*Source: Page 26, Chapter 6, Section 6.1 (KPI Table)*
